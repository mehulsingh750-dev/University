- 1960 Bill of Rights
	- Non entrenched statutory law
		- Its not in the written constitution
		- Simple law of parliament that can be amended
	- Only at federal level
	- No judicial enforcement mechanism
		- Not able to enforce judicial remedies
	- Parliamentary supremacy
- 1982 Charter
	- Entrenched Constitutional Law
		- Not easily amendable, requires the 7-50 rule
	- Both at federal and provincial level
		- Enforced on everyone 
	- Judicial enforcement mechanism 
		- Via Sec 52, 24
	- Constitutional and Judicial Supremacy
- New Brunswick is the only province to adopt official bilingualism
	- Right for every public servant to work in the official language of their choice
- Remedial Mechanism
	- Reasonable limits clause (Section 1)
		- Rights are not absolute, they are subject to parliament review and veto
		- Section 1 comes into play when the court rules a government action violates a right
		- They then decide if the infringement is justified
		- Oakes Test
			- The government must be pressing and substantial
			- The means must be reasonable and demonstrably justifiable 
				- Rational Connection
				- Minimal Impairment
				- Proportionality
