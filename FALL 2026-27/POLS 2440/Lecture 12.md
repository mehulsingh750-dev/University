- Powers of the Bureaucrat
	- The Bundesrat has an absolute veto power over some policies
	- Bundesrat is the upper house of the legislature
	- Known as 'Consent bills', includes anything that impacts the competences and budgets of the Lander
	- The Bundestag cannot overrule the Bundesrat on these issues
	- The Bundesrat considers all other bills as well
	- Known as 'Objection Bills', Bundestag can overrule the Bundesrat on these Bills

- France - The Fifth Republic and the Geographic Division of Power
	- The Fifth Republic is a unitary state
	- Power is traditionally centralized in Paris
	- An argument in favour of centralization is that is would strengthen national unity
	- Centralization would promote the equality of all French
	- In 1800 Napoleon introduced a system of 'Prefects', representatives of the central government 
	- This is an example of Deconcentrated government rather than decentralization
- Regional Divisions in France
	- National Identity is traditionally strong in France
	- But, there are regions which seek more power.
	- Decentralization has occurred in France starting in the 1980's
	- While France remains a unitary state, there are a large number of sub-national units of government
	- There are regional governments, departments and communes 
	- These governments have some degree of autonomy but are still subject to central control
	- Local Politicians are powerful political actors capable of influencing development at the centre
	- Members of the Senate are selected by an electoral college that includes local and regional politicians 
- French Senate
	- Senate is the Upper House 
	- Plays a role in constitutional amendments
	- Proposal adopted in identical terms by the senate and national assembly
	- Ratified by a joint session of the NA and the Senate (requires 3/5 majority)
	- Has a say on legislative proposals 
	- If the process fails to produce and agreement between the two chambers, the measure can be approved by the National Assembly alone
