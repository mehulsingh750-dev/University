- The German Basic Law
	- The Federal Republic of Germany's Constitution was drafted after the end of the Second World War and the unconditional surrender of the Nazi's
	- The Basic Law is the name given to the constitution of the Federal Republic of Germany - entered into effect of 1949
	- This constitution only applied to West Germany. The German Democratic Republic had its own constitution
	- With the reunification of Germany in 1990m the former territory of East Germany was integrated within the framework of the Basic Law.
	- There was a major economic difference between East and West Germany which caused the integration of them to be difficult
	- The East didn't have a voting system while the West did, making the political integration complicated as well
	- The Basic law was drafted under the influence of occupying powers
	- The primary goal of Basic Law was to establish a liberal democracy in West Germany that would survive and become a consolidated democracy
	- The first articles contain the fundamentals rights of German Citizens protected from being infringed on by the state - Negative rights.
	- It also contains positive rights
	- These rights cannot be revoked by an act of parliament or constitutional amendment - The Eternity Clause ****exam****
	- The negative rights include the rights to form political parties 
		- Except extreme parties, made to fundamentally overthrow the constitution and/or abolish the democratic order
		- The Federal Constitutional Court can rule these to be unconstitutional
	- Amending the Basic Law requires a SUPERMAJORITY
		- Anything beyond a simple majority
		- Requires a proposal from the federal government, the Bundesrat or the Bundestag
		- Approval requires a two thirds majority of the Bundestag and the Bundesrat
		- An example is the DEBT BRAKE (balanced budget) amendment passed in 2009. The limits the government's ability to run a budget deficit
		- Current German Chancellor pushed through a constitutional amendment in 2025
		- This reform allows for the increased defence spending as well as increased infrastructure 
	- The Federal Constitutional Court
		- Germany's Supreme Court
		- The court has been set up to be removed from politics (third party review). The 16 judges serve 12 year terms that cannot be renewed 
		- 8 are elected by the Bundestag and t8 by the Bundesrat. They need to secure the support of 2/3's of the votes cast
		- It has the power of JUDICIAL REVIEW; that is, it has the power to declare a federal legislative act unconstitutional
		- The court has the power of 'concrete review' and 'abstract review'
